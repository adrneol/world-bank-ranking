Capital Flow — FDI methodology
Core principle

FDI is a flow, not a stock.

So:

Do not compare the Start-year FDI flow to the End-year FDI flow and call that the amount of FDI attracted during the period.

That was the methodological problem with the earlier endpoint formula.

For a period from Start = S to End = E, I recommend defining the period as the E − S annual observations from S+1 through E.

So:

2004 → 2014 = 2005, 2006, ..., 2014

2014 → 2024 = 2015, 2016, ..., 2024

2004 → 2024 = 2005, ..., 2024

This gives exactly 10 years for a decade and prevents 2014 from being counted in both adjacent periods.

Let:

$$ F_{i,t} $$

= FDI net inflow of country \(i\) in year \(t\).

Let:

$$ R_{i,t} $$

= FDI net inflows as % of GDP.

And:

$$ N=E-S $$
Metric 1 — FDI Net Inflows
Basis A — Annual Net FDI
Meaning

How much net FDI entered the economy during that particular year.

$$ AnnualFDI_{i,t}=F_{i,t} $$

Unit:

current US$

Example:

India 2024 = whatever World Bank reports for India in 2024.

Ranking

Higher net inflow → higher rank.

$$ Rank_i=1+\#\{j:F_j>F_i\} $$

So:

+$100B ranks above +$50B
+$10B ranks above −$5B
−$5B ranks above −$20B

Do not rename this as "best FDI". It is simply larger net inflow.

Benchmark

Leave-one-out mean:

$$ Benchmark_i= \frac{\sum_{j\ne i}F_j}{n-1} $$

Relative difference:

$$ Gap_i=F_i-Benchmark_i $$

Unit:

current US$

Positive means the country's annual net FDI is above the eligible-economy average.

Basis B — Period Cumulative Net FDI

This should be your main period basis.

Because FDI is a flow, the economically correct way to measure how much net FDI accumulated as transactions over the period is to sum the annual flows.

$$ PeriodFDI_i= \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}F_{i,t} $$

For 2004→2014:

$$ F_{2005}+F_{2006}+\cdots+F_{2014} $$
What this means

It answers:

How much cumulative net FDI did this economy receive over the entire period?

This uses every year and therefore fixes the flaw in an endpoint-only comparison.

Ranking

Higher cumulative net inflow → higher rank.

$$ Rank_i=rank_{\downarrow}(PeriodFDI_i) $$
Benchmark
$$ Benchmark_i= \frac{\sum_{j\ne i}PeriodFDI_j}{n-1} $$

Gap:

$$ Gap_i=PeriodFDI_i-Benchmark_i $$

Unit:

current US$

Important

Call this:

Cumulative net FDI inflow

Do not call it:

FDI stock

because summing flows is not the same thing as the value of the foreign investment stock.

UNCTAD explicitly distinguishes annual FDI flows from accumulated FDI stock.

Basis C — Average Annual Net FDI

This is useful because it puts different period lengths on a common annual scale.

$$ AvgAnnualFDI_i= \frac{\sum_{t=S+1}^{E}F_{i,t}}{N} $$

For a 10-year interval:

$$ AvgAnnualFDI= \frac{F_{2005}+\cdots+F_{2014}}{10} $$

Unit:

current US$/year

Ranking

Higher average annual net FDI → higher rank.

Benchmark
$$ Benchmark_i= mean_{j\ne i}(AvgAnnualFDI_j) $$

Gap:

$$ Gap_i=AvgAnnualFDI_i-Benchmark_i $$
Important mathematical property

For a fixed period length, Basis B and Basis C have identical rankings because:

$$ AvgAnnualFDI_i=\frac{PeriodFDI_i}{N} $$

and \(N\) is identical for every country.

So they are not different ranking dimensions.

They answer different questions:

Basis B → total amount received across the period
Basis C → typical annual amount during the period

I would still keep both as bases because the displayed interpretation differs.

Should FDI Net have a "% growth" basis?
No — not as a canonical basis.

This is important.

A formula such as:

$$ \frac{F_E-F_S}{F_S}\times100 $$

is problematic for FDI because:

FDI can be negative.
FDI can be zero or very close to zero.
It can cross from positive to negative.
A huge percentage change can result from a tiny starting flow.
It ignores all the intermediate years.

For example:

$$ F_{2004}=1B $$

and

$$ F_{2014}=10B $$

would give +900%, even if the country received only $0.5B in many years in between.

Conversely:

$$ F_{2004}=10B,\quad F_{2014}=5B $$

gives −50%, even though the country might have received enormous cumulative FDI during 2005–2014.

And if:

$$ F_{2004}=-1B $$

the conventional percentage growth becomes economically awkward.

Therefore

Do not make "FDI % change from Start to End" a primary ranking basis.

You can optionally show:

Change in annual FDI flow

$$ \Delta FDI=F_E-F_S $$

but this should be clearly labeled as an endpoint diagnostic, not cumulative-period performance.

I would not rank countries on it as part of the canonical Capital Flow methodology.

Metric 2 — FDI Net Inflows (% of GDP)

This metric has a different purpose.

It answers:

How large are net FDI inflows relative to the size of the domestic economy?

World Bank defines the series exactly this way: FDI net inflows divided by GDP.

Let:

$$ R_{i,t}= \frac{FDI_{i,t}}{GDP_{i,t}}\times100 $$
Basis A — Annual FDI Net Inflows (% of GDP)

Direct World Bank value:

$$ AnnualRatio_{i,t}=R_{i,t} $$

Example:

India:

FDI net inflows = 1.8% of GDP

Ranking

Higher FDI/GDP ratio → higher rank.

Again, phrase it descriptively:

"Higher net FDI inflows relative to GDP"

rather than:

"better economy"

because the ratio itself is not a complete measure of economic performance.

Benchmark
$$ Benchmark_i= mean_{j\ne i}(R_{j,t}) $$

Gap:

$$ Gap_i=R_{i,t}-Benchmark_i $$

Unit:

percentage points

Basis B — Average Annual FDI Inflows (% of GDP)

For the period:

$$ AverageRatio_i= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}R_{i,t} $$

So for 2004→2014:

$$ \frac{ R_{2005}+R_{2006}+\cdots+R_{2014} }{10} $$
Meaning

It answers:

What was the country's typical annual FDI inflow relative to GDP during the period?

This is useful because a country could have:

10% in one year,
0.5% in several years,
1% in others,

and the average captures the overall annual intensity rather than merely looking at one endpoint.

Ranking

Higher average annual FDI/GDP → higher rank.

Benchmark
$$ Benchmark_i= mean_{j\ne i}(AverageRatio_j) $$

Gap:

$$ Gap_i=AverageRatio_i-Benchmark_i $$

Unit:

percentage points

Basis C — Cumulative FDI Relative to Cumulative GDP

This is the most important additional basis for the % of GDP metric.

Do not simply add the annual percentages:

$$ R_{2005}+R_{2006}+\cdots+R_{2014} $$

That would be mathematically convenient but economically misleading because each percentage has a different GDP denominator.

Instead calculate:

$$ CumulativeFDIShare_i= \frac{ \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}FDI_{i,t} }{ \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}GDP_{i,t} } \times100 $$

This gives:

Cumulative net FDI over the period as a percentage of cumulative GDP over the same period.

This is mathematically much more coherent.

Example

Suppose:

Year	FDI	GDP	FDI/GDP
1	$10B	$100B	10%
2	$5B	$200B	2.5%

Don't calculate:

$$ 10\%+2.5\%=12.5\% $$

Instead:

$$ \frac{10+5}{100+200}\times100 =5\% $$

The 5% is the economically meaningful cumulative-flow-to-cumulative-output relationship.

Ranking

Higher:

$$ \frac{\sum FDI}{\sum GDP} $$

→ higher rank.

Benchmark

Calculate each country's cumulative ratio first, then:

$$ Benchmark_i= mean_{j\ne i}(CumulativeFDIShare_j) $$

Do not calculate one "global cumulative FDI / global cumulative GDP" and compare every country to that as the benchmark if your existing system defines benchmark as the average of eligible economies. Those answer different questions.

Gap:

$$ Gap_i=CumulativeFDIShare_i-Benchmark_i $$

Unit:

percentage points

Basis D — Change in FDI/GDP Ratio

I would make this an optional descriptive basis, not a primary performance basis.

$$ \Delta Ratio_i=R_{i,E}-R_{i,S} $$

Unit:

percentage points

Example:

$$ 1.5\%\rightarrow2.2\% $$

means:

$$ +0.7\text{ percentage points} $$

This is far better than saying:

"FDI/GDP increased 46.7%"

because the ratio itself can be close to zero or negative.

For ratios, percentage-point change is the clean measure of movement.

I would display it, but I would not make it part of your main "period FDI performance" ranking because it is still only an endpoint measure.

Therefore the canonical design

I would define the family as:

Metric	Basis A	Basis B	Basis C	Optional D
FDI net inflows (US$)	Annual FDI	Cumulative period FDI	Average annual FDI	Endpoint change in annual FDI
FDI net inflows (% GDP)	Annual FDI/GDP	Average annual FDI/GDP	Cumulative FDI / cumulative GDP	Endpoint pp change

That is 3 canonical analytical bases per metric, plus 1 diagnostic movement basis.

I would not add a generic "% growth" basis to either metric.

Observed vs Like-for-Like

This part should be extremely strict.

Annual basis

For year \(t\):

Observed universe

Country must have valid FDI data for \(t\).

For FDI/GDP:

Country must have valid FDI/GDP data for \(t\), or valid FDI and GDP inputs if you derive the ratio.

Period FDI Net

For:

$$ S\rightarrow E $$

country must have valid FDI observations for:

$$ S+1,\ldots,E $$

every year.

Do not calculate a country's total from only the years that happen to exist.

Otherwise:

Country A could have 10 years of FDI.

Country B could have 6 years.

and B would appear artificially low simply because four years disappeared.

So:

Complete-period data required.

Period FDI/GDP Average

Same requirement:

$$ R_{S+1},...,R_E $$

must all exist.

Cumulative FDI / cumulative GDP

Require:

FDI for every year S+1...E
GDP for every year S+1...E

because both the numerator and denominator are period aggregates.

Like-for-like universe

This is where your existing architecture becomes important.

Suppose you compare:

2004→2014

against

2014→2024

For FDI-flow bases:

Period 1 requires

2005–2014

Period 2 requires

2015–2024

Therefore the like-for-like universe is countries with complete:

2005–2024

data.

So:

$$ LFL= Valid(2005,...,2014) \cap Valid(2015,...,2024) $$

which is equivalent to:

$$ Valid(2005,...,2024) $$

for these adjacent periods.

This means when you say:

"Compare India's FDI in the two decades like-for-like"

both decade calculations are recomputed using the same country universe.

That is exactly what you want.

Cross-globe comparison

The ranking universe should follow your existing structure:

All economies

Use all eligible economies satisfying that basis's data requirement.

Country-group

Filter to the documented country group first, then apply the period data requirement.

Do not:

rank globally,
then extract a regional subset.

Instead:

$$ CountryGroup \rightarrow DataEligibility \rightarrow Ranking $$

That keeps the denominator and benchmark internally consistent.

Ranking methodology
FDI US$

Higher net FDI:

$$ \text{rank 1} $$

For period cumulative:

$$ rank_{\downarrow} \left( \sum FDI \right) $$

For average annual:

$$ rank_{\downarrow} \left( \frac{\sum FDI}{N} \right) $$

Because the denominator \(N\) is identical across countries, the two rankings are mathematically identical.

FDI % GDP

Higher ratio:

$$ \text{rank 1} $$

For average:

$$ rank_{\downarrow} \left( \frac{\sum R_t}{N} \right) $$

For cumulative intensity:

$$ rank_{\downarrow} \left( \frac{\sum FDI_t} {\sum GDP_t} \times100 \right) $$

Use full precision for ranking, then round for display.

Use competition ranking consistently with your other metric families.

Very important: negative FDI

Do not do any of these:

$$ |FDI| $$

or

$$ \max(FDI,0) $$

or replace negative with zero.

A negative net inflow can represent disinvestment/reverse investment under the net-flow framework.

Therefore:

$$ -5B $$

must remain:

$$ -5B $$

and

$$ -5\% $$

must remain:

$$ -5\% $$

for the ratio metric.

Zero is also a valid economic observation, not missing data.

Benchmark methodology

For every basis:

FDI US$
$$ Gap_i=Value_i-\overline{Value}_{-i} $$

Interpretation:

+ → country's FDI is above peer average
− → below peer average
FDI % GDP
$$ Gap_i=Ratio_i-\overline{Ratio}_{-i} $$

Interpretation:

+2.4 pp → 2.4 percentage points above peer average
−1.1 pp → 1.1 percentage points below peer average

This is cleaner than using wording like "better than benchmark."

What I would explicitly prohibit in the implementation
❌ FDI endpoint growth as the main period metric
$$ (F_E/F_S-1) $$

Not robust because FDI is a volatile net flow and can be zero/negative.

❌ Sum of FDI/GDP percentages
$$ \sum(FDI/GDP) $$

Not a meaningful cumulative percentage.

❌ Endpoint FDI to represent decade FDI
$$ FDI_{2014}-FDI_{2004} $$

only measures the change in the annual flow, not the amount of FDI received during the decade.

❌ Treating cumulative FDI as an FDI stock
$$ \sum FDI_t \neq FDI\ Stock $$
❌ Dropping negative observations

Negative net FDI is legitimate data.

❌ Treating missing as zero

Missing observation ≠ zero flow.

Final methodology structure

I would freeze the Capital Flow family as:

FDI Net Inflows

A — Annual Net FDI
Raw annual WDI flow.

B — Cumulative Net FDI

$$ \sum_{S+1}^{E}FDI_t $$

C — Average Annual Net FDI

$$ \frac{\sum_{S+1}^{E}FDI_t}{E-S} $$

D — Annual-flow change (diagnostic only)

$$ FDI_E-FDI_S $$

Not a canonical period-performance rank.

FDI Net Inflows (% of GDP)

A — Annual FDI/GDP
Raw WDI ratio.

B — Average Annual FDI/GDP

$$ \frac{1}{E-S}\sum_{S+1}^{E}R_t $$

C — Cumulative FDI / Cumulative GDP

$$ \frac{\sum FDI_t}{\sum GDP_t}\times100 $$

D — Change in FDI/GDP (diagnostic only)

$$ R_E-R_S $$

expressed in percentage points, not percent growth.

This gives you a genuinely flow-aware methodology: the dollar metric answers how much capital flowed, while the GDP-normalized metric answers how large that flow was relative to the economy, and both use the full period rather than pretending the two endpoint observations describe the intervening decade. The underlying World Bank definitions support this distinction.
