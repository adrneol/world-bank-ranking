GDP Deflator Inflation — Final Methodology Specification
1. Metric definition
World Bank indicator

Indicator code: NY.GDP.DEFL.KD.ZG
Indicator name: Inflation, GDP deflator (annual % growth)
Unit: Percent
Frequency: Annual

The World Bank defines GDP-deflator inflation as the annual growth rate of the GDP implicit deflator, which measures the rate of price change in the economy as a whole. The underlying GDP implicit deflator is defined as the ratio of GDP at current prices to GDP at constant prices. The WDI metadata identifies the source as national accounts data from national statistical authorities, central banks, World Bank staff estimates, and related national-accounts sources.

Conceptually:

$$ GDPDeflator_t= \frac{GDP^{current}_t}{GDP^{constant}_t} $$

and annual GDP-deflator inflation is the annual growth of that implicit deflator:

$$ D_{i,t} = \left( \frac{Deflator_{i,t}} {Deflator_{i,t-1}} -1 \right)\times100 $$

where:

\(i\) = economy
\(t\) = year
\(D_{i,t}\) = GDP-deflator inflation in year \(t\)

Thus, a value such as India's 5.73% in 2004 means that the GDP implicit price level increased by approximately 5.73% from the preceding year to 2004.

GDP-deflator inflation is therefore different from CPI inflation. CPI inflation measures annual price change for a consumer basket, while GDP-deflator inflation measures annual price change for the economy-wide GDP implicit price level.

2. Purpose of the Movement methodology

The Movement system should answer three distinct questions:

Basis 1 — Annual GDP-deflator inflation

What was the economy-wide GDP price inflation rate in a particular year?

Basis 2 — Average annual GDP-deflator inflation

What was the country's average annual GDP-deflator inflation rate during a selected period?

Basis 3 — Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation

By what total percentage did the GDP implicit price level increase across the selected period?

These are three different economic measurements.

They must not be combined into a generic "growth" calculation.

3. Approved bases

The GDP-deflator inflation metric has exactly three Movement bases:

Annual GDP-deflator inflation (%)
Average annual GDP-deflator inflation (%)
Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation (%)

A fourth basis such as "change in GDP-deflator inflation rate" is not used.

In particular, the following is not an approved Movement basis:

$$ D_{i,E}-D_{i,S} $$

That would measure the change in the inflation rate itself, not the change in the GDP price level.

Likewise:

$$ \frac{D_{i,E}-D_{i,S}}{D_{i,S}}\times100 $$

must not be presented as GDP-deflator inflation growth.

4. Critical time-interval rule

GDP-deflator inflation is an annual growth rate.

Therefore, for a period:

$$ S\rightarrow E $$

the annual inflation observations representing the price transitions inside that interval are:

$$ S+1,S+2,\ldots,E $$

There are:

$$ \boxed{E-S} $$

annual transitions.

Example

For:

$$ 2004\rightarrow2014 $$

the required GDP-deflator inflation observations are:

$$ 2005,2006,\ldots,2014 $$

That is:

$$ 2014-2004=10 $$

annual inflation observations.

The 2004 inflation observation is not included in the 2004→2014 period calculation because 2004 inflation describes the inflation transition into 2004.

This rule is essential for avoiding off-by-one errors.

5. Basis 1 — Annual GDP-deflator inflation
Definition

For country \(i\) and selected year \(t\):

$$ \boxed{ A_{i,t}=D_{i,t} } $$

No transformation is required.

The backend uses the World Bank's annual GDP-deflator inflation observation directly.

Example — India

From the project's annual table:

Year	GDP-deflator inflation
2004	5.73%
2014	3.33%
2024	2.47%

Therefore:

India's 2004 annual GDP-deflator inflation = 5.73%
India's 2014 annual GDP-deflator inflation = 3.33%
India's 2024 annual GDP-deflator inflation = 2.47%

These are independent annual observations.

Do not calculate a 2004→2014 movement for Basis 1 by subtracting 3.33 from 5.73.

6. Basis 1 — Cross-sectional ranking

For each selected year \(t\), construct the valid economy universe:

$$ U_t= \{i:D_{i,t}\text{ is valid}\} $$

Then calculate the annual GDP-deflator inflation value for every eligible economy.

For example:

Economy	2004 GDP-deflator inflation
A	2.0%
B	3.5%
India	5.73%
C	7.0%
D	10.0%

Rank directly by:

$$ \boxed{\text{ascending annual GDP-deflator inflation}} $$

Thus:

Economy	Inflation	Rank
A	2.0%	#1
B	3.5%	#2
India	5.73%	#3
C	7.0%	#4
D	10.0%	#5

The denominator is the number of eligible economies in that exact year's universe.

7. Basis 1 — Cross-sectional PP benchmark

PP is not a time-series calculation.

It is calculated across economies within the same year.

For each economy \(i\):

$$ \overline{A}_{-i,t} = \frac{ \sum_{j\in U_t,j\ne i} A_{j,t} }{ |U_t|-1 } $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ PP_{i,t} = \overline{A}_{-i,t}-A_{i,t} } $$

where the country's own value is excluded from its benchmark.

Example

Suppose in 2004:

Economy A = 2%
Economy B = 4%
India = 6%
Economy C = 8%
Economy D = 10%

The average of economies other than India is:

$$ \frac{2+4+8+10}{4}=6\% $$

India:

$$ 6\% $$

Therefore:

$$ PP_{India}=6-6=0.00\text{ pp} $$

Now suppose India were 7% instead:

$$ PP_{India}=6-7=-1.00\text{ pp} $$

Interpretation:

India's annual GDP-deflator inflation was 1.00 percentage point higher than the average of the other eligible economies in 2004.

8. PP-based ranking logic

The system calculates the PP for every eligible economy, not just India.

For every economy:

$$ PP_i=\overline{A}_{-i}-A_i $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{\text{Rank by highest PP}} $$

Because the leave-one-out PP is mathematically monotonic with lower own inflation, this produces the same ordering as ranking by lowest annual GDP-deflator inflation.

Therefore:

Rank = direct cross-sectional position based on the basis-specific metric.

PP = benchmark context relative to the average of the other eligible economies.

The system must not use India's PP against itself to create a rank.

9. Basis 2 — Average annual GDP-deflator inflation
Definition

This basis asks:

What was the average annual GDP-deflator inflation rate during the selected interval?

For country \(i\), from start \(S\) to end \(E\):

$$ \boxed{ M_i= \frac{1}{E-S} \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}D_{i,t} } $$

The denominator is \(E-S\), the number of annual inflation transitions in the interval.

10. Basis 2 — Example

Suppose a fictional country has:

Year	GDP-deflator inflation
2005	5%
2006	0%
2007	10%

For:

$$ 2004\rightarrow2007 $$

there are:

$$ 2007-2004=3 $$

annual observations relevant to the interval:

2005, 2006, 2007.

Therefore:

$$ M= \frac{5+0+10}{3} $$ $$ \boxed{M=5.00\%} $$

Interpretation:

The country's GDP-deflator inflation rate averaged 5.00% per year during the 2004→2007 interval.

It does not mean the GDP price level increased by 5%.

That is Basis 3.

11. India example — Basis 2

For India's 2004→2014 period, use the annual GDP-deflator inflation observations:

Year	GDP-deflator inflation
2005	5.62%
2006	8.40%
2007	6.94%
2008	9.19%
2009	7.04%
2010	10.53%
2011	8.73%
2012	7.93%
2013	6.19%
2014	3.33%

Then:

$$ M_{India} = \frac{ 5.62+8.40+6.94+9.19+7.04+ 10.53+8.73+7.93+6.19+3.33 }{10} $$ $$ \boxed{M_{India}=7.39\%} $$

Therefore:

India's average annual GDP-deflator inflation during 2004→2014 was 7.39%.

12. Basis 2 — Cross-sectional ranking

First calculate \(M_i\) separately for every eligible economy.

For each country:

$$ M_i= \frac{1}{E-S} \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}D_{i,t} $$

Then form the cross-sectional period universe.

Rank:

$$ \boxed{\text{ascending }M_i} $$

Example:

Economy	2004→2014 average	Rank
A	2.50%	#1
B	4.10%	#2
India	7.39%	#3
C	9.00%	#4
D	12.00%	#5

Thus the rank is based on the calculated period-average value of every country, not India's value compared only with a world average.

13. Basis 2 — PP benchmark

After every country's period average has been calculated:

$$ \overline{M}_{-i} = \frac{ \sum_{j\ne i}M_j }{ N-1 } $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ PP_i=\overline{M}_{-i}-M_i } $$
Example

Suppose:

India:

$$ M_{India}=7.39\% $$

Average of all other eligible economies:

$$ \overline{M}_{-India}=6.00\% $$

Then:

$$ PP_{India}=6.00-7.39 $$ $$ \boxed{-1.39\text{ pp}} $$

Interpretation:

India's average annual GDP-deflator inflation was 1.39 percentage points higher than the average of the other eligible economies during the selected period.

Again, PP is cross-sectional.

It is not:

$$ D_{2014}-D_{2004} $$
14. Basis 3 — Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation

This basis asks:

By what total percentage did the GDP implicit price level increase over the selected period?

Annual inflation compounds.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ C_i= \left[ \prod_{t=S+1}^{E} \left(1+\frac{D_{i,t}}{100}\right) -1 \right]\times100 } $$

This is the cumulative percentage increase in the GDP implicit price level over the selected interval.

15. Basis 3 — Why annual rates cannot simply be added

Suppose:

Year	GDP-deflator inflation
2005	5%
2006	0%
2007	10%

Incorrect:

$$ 5+0+10=15\% $$

Correct:

$$ (1.05)(1.00)(1.10)-1 $$ $$ =1.155-1 $$ $$ \boxed{15.50\%} $$

So:

Average annual inflation = 5.00%

but:

Cumulative inflation = 15.50%

These are different economic quantities.

16. India example — Basis 3

For India's 2004→2014 period:

$$ C_{India} = (1+0.0562) (1+0.0840) (1+0.0694) (1+0.0919) (1+0.0704) $$ $$ \times (1+0.1053) (1+0.0873) (1+0.0793) (1+0.0619) (1+0.0333)-1 $$

Using the annual values displayed in the project's table:

$$ \boxed{C_{India}\approx103.67\%} $$

Interpretation:

India's GDP implicit price level increased by approximately 103.67% from the 2004 level to the 2014 level, based on compounding the annual GDP-deflator inflation observations from 2005 through 2014.

This does not mean India's GDP real output doubled.

It means the implicit price level associated with GDP increased by approximately that amount.

17. Basis 3 — Cross-sectional ranking

First calculate cumulative GDP-deflator inflation independently for every eligible economy:

$$ C_i= \left[ \prod_{t=S+1}^{E} \left(1+\frac{D_{i,t}}{100}\right) -1 \right]\times100 $$

Then rank:

$$ \boxed{\text{ascending }C_i} $$

Example:

Economy	Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation	Rank
A	20%	#1
B	45%	#2
India	103.67%	#3
C	130%	#4
D	200%	#5

The rank is therefore based on the cumulative price increase calculated separately for every economy.

18. Basis 3 — PP benchmark

After calculating \(C_i\) for every eligible economy:

$$ \overline{C}_{-i} = \frac{ \sum_{j\ne i}C_j }{ N-1 } $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ PP_i=\overline{C}_{-i}-C_i } $$
Example

Suppose:

India:

$$ C_{India}=103.67\% $$

Other-economy average:

$$ \overline{C}_{-India}=80.00\% $$

Then:

$$ PP_{India}=80.00-103.67 $$ $$ \boxed{-23.67\text{ pp}} $$

Interpretation:

India's cumulative GDP-deflator inflation was 23.67 percentage points higher than the average cumulative inflation of the other eligible economies.

19. Ranking architecture — common to all three bases

The backend must follow this sequence.

Step 1 — Determine the relevant universe

For the selected basis and period, identify all economies with valid data required by that basis.

Step 2 — Calculate the basis-specific value for every economy

Do not calculate India's value first and then compare it with the average.

Every economy must receive its own independently calculated value.

Basis 1
$$ X_i=D_{i,t} $$
Basis 2
$$ X_i= \frac{1}{E-S} \sum_{t=S+1}^{E}D_{i,t} $$
Basis 3
$$ X_i= \left[ \prod_{t=S+1}^{E} (1+D_{i,t}/100)-1 \right]\times100 $$
Step 3 — Rank the complete cross-section

For all economies in the universe:

$$ Rank_i=\text{ascending }X_i $$

Lower GDP-deflator inflation means a lower rank number.

Step 4 — Calculate the focus country's leave-one-out benchmark

For the focus country \(i\):

$$ \overline{X}_{-i} = \frac{ \sum_{j\ne i}X_j }{ N-1 } $$
Step 5 — Calculate PP
$$ \boxed{ PP_i=\overline{X}_{-i}-X_i } $$
Step 6 — Present the focus country

The UI can therefore show:

Focus-country value
Eligible economy count
Focus-country rank
Average of other eligible economies
Focus-country relative gap in percentage points
20. Important distinction: rank vs PP

The two outputs serve different purposes.

Rank

Answers:

Where does this economy stand among the eligible economies?

PP

Answers:

How far is this economy's value from the average of the other eligible economies?

Example:

India — 7.39%
Rank — #45 / 180
Other-economy average — 6.00%
Relative gap — −1.39 percentage points

The rank and PP are related, but they are not the same statistic.

The rank is based on the cross-sectional ordering.

The PP is the benchmark comparison.

21. Observed universe

The observed universe is determined according to the data actually required by the chosen basis.

Basis 1 — Annual GDP-deflator inflation

For year \(t\):

$$ \boxed{ U_t= \{i:D_{i,t}\text{ is valid}\} } $$

For example:

2004 might contain 204 valid economies
2014 might contain 210
2024 might contain 200

There is no requirement that these counts be identical.

The ranking for each year uses that year's own valid universe.

Basis 2 — Average annual GDP-deflator inflation

For interval \(S\rightarrow E\), an economy is eligible only if every required annual observation exists:

$$ \boxed{ U^{Avg}_{S,E} = \{i: D_{i,S+1},D_{i,S+2},...,D_{i,E} \text{ are all valid} \} } $$

If one required year is missing, exclude that economy from the period calculation.

Do not:

average only the years that happen to exist;
interpolate missing observations;
substitute another measure.

This is necessary because otherwise different countries would be averaged over different numbers of annual observations.

Basis 3 — Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation

The same completeness requirement applies:

$$ \boxed{ U^{Cum}_{S,E} = \{i: D_{i,S+1},...,D_{i,E} \text{ are all valid} \} } $$

Every annual transition needed by the compounding calculation must be available.

22. Like-for-like universe

Like-for-like does not change the basis formula.

It changes the set of economies included in the cross-section.

This distinction is important.

Basis 1 — Like-for-like

With:

$$ Start=2004 $$ $$ Middle=2014 $$ $$ End=2024 $$

take:

$$ \boxed{ L^{Annual} = U_{2004}\cap U_{2014}\cap U_{2024} } $$

Every economy in this universe has valid annual GDP-deflator inflation in all three selected years.

That same universe is then used for:

2004 ranking
2014 ranking
2024 ranking

This allows those annual cross-sectional positions to be compared using the same economies.

23. Like-for-like for Basis 2 and Basis 3

For:

$$ 2004\rightarrow2014 $$

the required annual observations are:

$$ 2005,\ldots,2014 $$

For:

$$ 2014\rightarrow2024 $$

the required annual observations are:

$$ 2015,\ldots,2024 $$

Therefore, if the goal is to compare the two periods using exactly the same economies, the like-for-like universe must contain countries with complete required data for both periods.

Equivalently, those countries must have all required annual observations across:

$$ 2005,\ldots,2024 $$

Then:

Period 1

Calculate each country's 2004→2014 basis value using the common universe.

Period 2

Calculate each country's 2014→2024 basis value using the same common universe.

Full period

Calculate each country's 2004→2024 basis value using that same common universe.

29. Relationship between Basis 2 and Basis 3

This distinction must be preserved throughout the UI.

Suppose annual GDP-deflator inflation is:

$$ 5\%,0\%,10\% $$

Then:

Average annual inflation
$$ \frac{5+0+10}{3} = 5\% $$
Cumulative inflation
$$ (1.05)(1.00)(1.10)-1 = 15.5\% $$

Therefore:

5% answers "what was the average annual inflation rate?"

while:

15.5% answers "how much did the GDP price level rise in total?"

Neither is a substitute for the other.

The PP architecture is identical, but the underlying value changes.

Basis 1

Suppose India's annual GDP-deflator inflation:

$$ D_{India}=3.33\% $$

and other-economy average:

$$ \bar D_{-India}=4.00\% $$

Then:

$$ PP=4.00-3.33 $$ $$ \boxed{+0.67\text{ pp}} $$

Interpretation:

India's annual GDP-deflator inflation was 0.67 percentage points lower than the average of the other eligible economies.

Basis 2

Suppose India's period average:

$$ M_{India}=7.39\% $$

and other-economy average:

$$ \bar M_{-India}=6.00\% $$

Then:

$$ PP=6.00-7.39 $$ $$ \boxed{-1.39\text{ pp}} $$

Interpretation:

India's average annual GDP-deflator inflation was 1.39 percentage points higher than the average of the other eligible economies.

Basis 3

Suppose India's cumulative inflation:

$$ C_{India}=103.67\% $$

and other-economy average:

$$ \bar C_{-India}=80.00\% $$

Then:

$$ PP=80.00-103.67 $$ $$ \boxed{-23.67\text{ pp}} $$

Interpretation:

India's cumulative GDP-deflator inflation was 23.67 percentage points higher than the average cumulative inflation of the other eligible economies.

31. PP interpretation rule

For all three bases:

$$ \boxed{ PP_i=\text{other-economy average}-\text{country value} } $$

Therefore:

Positive PP

The country's inflation measure is lower than the average of the other eligible economies.

Zero PP

The country's inflation measure equals the average of the other eligible economies.

Negative PP

The country's inflation measure is higher than the average of the other eligible economies.

The UI should not describe this as a generalized measure of whether an economy is "better" or "worse."

It is specifically a relative inflation-position measure.

32. Ranking direction

All three bases use:

$$ \boxed{\text{lower inflation} \rightarrow \text{rank 1}} $$

because each basis measures a form of price increase:

Basis 1

Lower annual GDP-deflator inflation.

Basis 2

Lower average annual GDP-deflator inflation.

Basis 3

Lower cumulative GDP-deflator inflation.

The UI should use metric-specific language.

Recommended:

Ranked by lower annual GDP-deflator inflation

Ranked by lower average annual GDP-deflator inflation

Ranked by lower cumulative GDP-deflator inflation

Avoid generic language such as:

"Best economy"

because the ranking only describes the selected inflation metric.

33. Rank denominator

The denominator must always equal the number of economies in the exact universe used for that calculation.

For example:

$$ \boxed{\#38/197} $$

means the country ranked 38th within a universe of 197 eligible economies.

For observed and like-for-like calculations, the denominator can therefore differ.

This is intentional.

34. Observed versus like-for-like
Observed

Use whatever economies have valid data required for that individual comparison.

Therefore:

$$ N_{Observed,1} $$

can differ from:

$$ N_{Observed,2} $$

and:

$$ N_{Observed,Full} $$
Like-for-like

Use one common universe across the relevant comparisons.

Therefore:

$$ N_{LFL,1} = N_{LFL,2} = N_{LFL,Full} $$

within the same selected methodology and comparison configuration.

This is the reason your site can display two sections:

Observed comparison

and

Like-for-like comparison

without changing the underlying economic formula.

The difference is the comparison universe, not the basis calculation.

35. Country-group filter

If the project has an existing documented country-group registry, the same logic can be applied.

First restrict the candidate universe to the relevant country group.

Then apply the required data-validity rule.

For example:

$$ Candidate= \text{countries in selected group} $$

then:

$$ Eligible= Candidate\cap ValidData $$

Do not invent a new economic classification specifically for this metric.

36. Benchmark terminology

Do not call:

$$ \bar X_{-India} $$

"World GDP-deflator inflation" or "global GDP-deflator inflation."

It is not a GDP-weighted world aggregate.

Instead use:

All-economy mode

Average GDP-deflator inflation of other eligible economies

Country-group mode

Average GDP-deflator inflation of other eligible economies in the selected group

This is a cross-sectional arithmetic mean of the eligible economy-level metric values.

37. No weighting unless explicitly specified

The benchmark:

$$ \frac{\sum X_j}{N-1} $$

is an unweighted arithmetic mean across eligible economies.

Do not weight the benchmark by:

GDP;
population;
PPP GDP;
country size;
trade share;

unless a separate methodology explicitly defines such weighting.

The purpose of this benchmark is to compare an individual economy against the average economy-level observation in the selected universe.

38. Precision rules

All calculations must use full backend precision.

Suppose two countries have:

$$ 7.391827\% $$

and:

$$ 7.392104\% $$

even if the UI displays:

$$ 7.39\% $$

for both, they must not automatically be treated as tied for ranking.

Ranking must use the unrounded backend values.

Rounding happens only at presentation time.

39. Tie rule

Use competition ranking.

Example:

Value	Rank
2.00%	#1
2.00%	#1
3.00%	#3
4.00%	#4

Do not force unique rankings using country names or another arbitrary secondary ordering when the underlying metric values are equal.

40. Negative GDP-deflator inflation

GDP-deflator inflation can be negative.

For example:

$$ D_t=-2\% $$

means the GDP implicit price level decreased relative to the previous year.

The methodology must therefore allow:

positive inflation;
zero inflation;
negative inflation.

The formulas remain valid.

For cumulative inflation:

$$ (1-0.02) $$

is simply:

$$ 0.98 $$

and can be compounded normally with the other annual factors.

41. Important economic interpretation

GDP-deflator inflation should not be interpreted as identical to household inflation.

The GDP deflator is a broad national-accounts price measure covering the price movement embodied in GDP. The World Bank explicitly describes it as the rate of price change in the economy as a whole.

Therefore:

CPI inflation and GDP-deflator inflation can legitimately differ.

The project should not describe one as an error-corrected version of the other.

They are different price measures answering different questions.

42. Validation relationship for Basis 3

Because GDP-deflator inflation is the annual growth rate of the GDP implicit deflator, compounding the annual rates over an interval gives the cumulative growth of that implicit price level, subject to the consistency of the underlying annual series. The World Bank defines the GDP implicit deflator as current-price GDP divided by constant-price GDP and its inflation series as the annual growth of that deflator.

Therefore Basis 3 provides a mathematically coherent cumulative measure:

$$ \boxed{ Cumulative= \prod(1+\text{annual rate})-1 } $$

It should not be replaced with the arithmetic sum of annual rates.

43. What the system must NOT do
Do not calculate cumulative inflation by addition

Wrong:

$$ 5+6+7=18\% $$

Correct:

$$ (1.05)(1.06)(1.07)-1 $$
Do not include the start-year inflation observation in the period transition

For:

$$ 2004\rightarrow2014 $$

use:

$$ 2005\rightarrow2014 $$

annual inflation observations.

Do not use 2004→2014 inclusive as eleven inflation observations.

Do not calculate a percentage growth of the inflation rate

Do not use:

$$ \frac{D_E-D_S}{D_S}\times100 $$

as a primary GDP-deflator Movement basis.

Do not use endpoint inflation rates to calculate cumulative inflation

For cumulative inflation, use the entire sequence:

$$ D_{S+1},...,D_E $$

not merely \(D_S\) and \(D_E\).

Do not calculate PP from two years of the same country

Wrong concept:

$$ D_{India,2014}-D_{India,2004} $$

Correct PP:

$$ PP_{India} = Average\ of\ other\ eligible\ economies - India's\ basis-specific\ value $$
Do not calculate India's PP first and derive the ranking only from India

Calculate the basis-specific metric for every eligible economy, calculate the cross-sectional ranking, then calculate India's leave-one-out benchmark.

Do not allow different universes inside one comparison

For one observed comparison, every country's rank and PP must be based on the same eligible economy universe.

For like-for-like comparison, every period must use the same intersection universe.

Do not invent missing annual observations

No interpolation or substitution unless a separately documented project-wide data policy explicitly permits it.

44. Complete mathematical specification

Let:

$$ D_{i,t} $$

denote GDP-deflator inflation for economy \(i\) in year \(t\).

Basis 1
$$ \boxed{ X^{(1)}_{i,t}=D_{i,t} } $$

Universe:

46. Final frozen specification

Metric: NY.GDP.DEFL.KD.ZG — Inflation, GDP deflator (annual % growth). The World Bank defines it as the annual growth rate of the GDP implicit deflator, which measures price change in the economy as a whole.

Basis 1 — Annual GDP-deflator inflation

$$ D_{i,t} $$

Uses the raw annual World Bank observation.

Basis 2 — Average annual GDP-deflator inflation

$$ \frac{1}{E-S}\sum_{t=S+1}^{E}D_{i,t} $$

Uses all annual inflation observations belonging to the selected interval.

Basis 3 — Cumulative GDP-deflator inflation

$$ \left[ \prod_{t=S+1}^{E} \left(1+\frac{D_{i,t}}{100}\right)-1 \right]\times100 $$

Compounds the annual inflation rates across the interval.

Ranking: Calculate the basis-specific value independently for every eligible economy, then perform a cross-sectional ascending ranking across that universe.

PP: For every economy, calculate:

$$ \boxed{ PP_i= Average\ of\ other\ eligible\ economies - Country_i } $$

with the focus country's own observation excluded from its benchmark.

Observed: Use the economies satisfying the validity requirements for that exact basis/comparison.

Like-for-like: Restrict the calculation to the intersection of the required valid economies so that the same economies are used across the selected comparison periods.

Start / Middle / End: With 2004 / 2014 / 2024, analyze 2004→2014, 2014→2024, and 2004→2024.

Direction: Lower annual, average annual, or cumulative GDP-deflator inflation receives the better numerical rank position.

Precision: Full backend precision for calculations, ranking and PP; rounding only for presentation.

Tie rule: Competition ranking.

No fourth basis: Do not introduce "change in GDP-deflator inflation rate" as a Movement basis.

This now mirrors the same cross-sectional ranking architecture you established for CPI inflation, while keeping the three GDP-deflator bases mathematically distinct. The key implementation rule is: first compute the appropriate metric for every country; then rank that cross-section; then calculate the focus country's leave-one-out PP against the same universe. The basis determines what each country's number means; Observed vs Like-for-like determines which countries participate.
